Reading is fast. Your eye jumps ahead, fills in the words it expects, and moves on. That is useful for getting through a page and useless for learning how the page was made.

Copying by hand slows you to the speed the writer worked at. You meet every comma. You feel where a sentence runs long and where it stops short. Writers have learned this way for centuries: Benjamin Franklin rebuilt essays from memory to see where his versions fell short, and Hunter S. Thompson typed out *The Great Gatsby* to feel what writing it was like.

## What changes

After a few days you start to hear sentences before you read them. You notice when your own writing leans on the same three rhythms. That is the point of the ten days: not to imitate, but to widen what your hand knows how to do.

## Why not type it

Typing is too fast and too forgiving. You can type a sentence without reading it. You cannot write one by hand that way.
